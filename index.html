Per-device delay

Every room, perfectly in sync.

Bluetooth speakers, TVs and Wi-Fi PCs each add their own lag, and in a whole-home setup that lag turns into an echo between rooms. Open any device in WavWiz and its speaker output gets its own live delay slider from 0 to 1000 ms, paired with a number box for exact values. Adjust it while the music plays and that room falls into line, so walking through the house sounds like one big room.

  • A live 0–1000 ms delay slider and number box for each device
  • Fine-tune while music plays, no restart needed
  • Calibrate and delay history buttons right next to the slider
  • Bluetooth, TVs, Wi-Fi PCs and wired speakers all line up

Your WavWiz server shows up on iPhone, iPad and Mac as the AirPlay speaker WavWiz – Whole House. Send audio from any app, like Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube or podcasts, and it plays in every room in sync, with title and artwork on Now Playing and the visualizers reacting to the music.

How it works

Three steps to whole-home sound.

  1. 01

    Install WavWiz on the PC that stays on

    Download the free Windows installer and choose WavWiz Server on the PC that holds your music. That PC runs the music for the whole house.

  2. 02

    Point it at your music folder

    Open Settings and add the folder that holds your music, on a local drive or a network share. WavWiz scans it and your artists, albums and genres show up in the Library.

  3. 03

    Open the player on every device

    PCs with speakers run WavWiz Player. Phones, tablets and TVs just open the WavWiz address in a web browser. Press play and every room starts on the same beat.

System requirements

What you need.

One Windows PC runs the house. Everything else just needs a web browser on your home network.

Server PC

  • 64-bit Windows 10 (October 2018 Update or newer) or Windows 11
  • A PC that stays on, ideally the one that holds your music
  • Music on local drives or network shares the PC can reach
  • Admin rights to install. Everything else comes in the installer.

Listening devices

  • Any modern web browser on a PC, Mac, phone, tablet or TV
  • Windows PCs with speakers can run WavWiz Player
  • Same home network as the server, set to Private in Windows

AirPlay & Spotify Connect

  • AirPlay from an iPhone, iPad or Mac on the same home Wi-Fi
  • Spotify Connect is optional, off by default, and needs your own Spotify Premium account

The WavWiz Player window uses Microsoft Edge WebView2, which comes with Windows 11 and current Windows 10. If it's missing, the player opens in your default browser instead.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is WavWiz really free?

Yes. WavWiz is free and open source under the MIT license. There's nothing to buy, no subscription and no cloud account to sign up for. The source code is in the WavWiz GitHub repository.

What speakers and devices does WavWiz work with?

The ones you already own. Windows PCs run WavWiz Player and play through whatever speakers are connected to them, wired or Bluetooth. Phones, tablets, Macs and TVs with a modern web browser just open the WavWiz address. You can also send audio to WavWiz from an iPhone, iPad or Mac with AirPlay, or from Spotify with the optional Spotify Connect feature (requires your own Spotify Premium account).

Do I need special hardware?

No. You need one 64-bit Windows 10 or Windows 11 PC that stays on, ideally the one that holds your music. Every other device just needs a web browser on the same home network. There are no proprietary multi-room speakers or hubs to buy. See the full system requirements.

Does WavWiz work with music on a NAS?

Yes. In Settings, add any music folder the PC can reach, on a local drive or a network share such as \\NAS\Music. If the share asks for a user name and password, save it first under Settings > Library > Network share logins (NAS). WavWiz scans the folder, and your artists, albums and genres show up in the Library.

Can I control WavWiz from my phone?

Yes, with no app to install. Open the WavWiz address in your phone's web browser while it's on the same Wi-Fi as the server. You can control playback and volume, add songs to the shared queue, and even play the music on the phone itself. If the phone asks, complete the one-time pairing.

Will every room stay in sync? What if one lags behind?

WavWiz keeps every speaker on the same beat, so walking between rooms sounds like one big room. Bluetooth speakers and TVs add their own lag, though. If one room sounds behind, open that device in WavWiz and move its delay slider (0 to 1000 ms) while the music plays until the echo disappears. See how per-device delay works.

Why does Windows SmartScreen warn me when I install WavWiz?

WavWiz is a public beta and its installer isn't code-signed yet, so SmartScreen doesn't recognize it. If you downloaded it from the official WavWiz releases page on GitHub, click More info, then Run anyway. To double-check the file, compare its SHA-256 checksum with the one listed on the release page. Only download WavWiz from the official repository.

Where do I report bugs or ask questions?

Report bugs on GitHub Issues. For questions, ideas and setup help, join WavWiz Discussions on GitHub. The release notes list what changed in the latest build.
