Multi-zone mesh audio wizardry, for gear old and new.

One mesh for every speaker you own.

WavWiz links Windows PCs, phones, and streamers and receivers with AirPlay or Cast into one free multi-room system, with no cloud and no lock-in. Native Linux and Raspberry Pi players are planned.

How it works

Four steps to multi-zone sound.

  1. 01

    Install it on one PC

    Install it on a Windows 10 or 11 PC that stays on, or run the optional Linux or Docker server. Point it at a folder or NAS share if you have a library — it’s optional.

  2. 02

    Add your speakers

    Use what you already own. Works with what you own shows what plays today, what’s in testing, and what’s planned.

  3. 03

    Make zones and line them up

    Group speakers into zones. Set each speaker’s delay by ear, or use Calibrate speakers with a PC mic. AirPlay and Cast speakers are set by ear for now.

  4. 04

    Play from anywhere

    Play your library, internet radio, or AirPlay from an iPhone, iPad or Mac to one zone or to “WavWiz – Everywhere.” Your phone is the remote.

Zones

Multi-room

A different playlist in every zone

Group speakers into zones like Kitchen or Office. Each zone can play its own queue, and you can drag songs into any of them from the Library or your phone.

Link zones for one sound

Link zones to share one queue and play in sync, or split a zone off when it wants its own music. AirPlay from an iPhone, iPad or Mac to one zone, or to “WavWiz – Everywhere.” Scenes, schedules and a Jukebox zone cover the rest.

Features

What it does

  1. Devices stay in sync

    Every device plays the same stream against a shared clock. Each speaker has its own volume and a 0–1000 ms delay you can set live. Calibrate speakers can measure WavWiz Players with a PC mic or phone; AirPlay and Cast are set by ear for now.

  2. Your library and radio

    Local folders and NAS shares, internet radio, playlists, gapless albums and ReplayGain/R128 leveling. Rip CDs to FLAC on the server PC; tag lookups wait for your approval. Optional Subsonic or Navidrome libraries too.

  3. Live sources

    In the Windows Player, stream that PC’s own sound or its microphone to any zone. New in 0.7.2. See What’s new.

  4. One house, one queue

    Everyone can add songs from a phone. Save scenes, run schedules and alarms, or give guest requests a Jukebox zone.

  5. AirPlay in

    iPhone, iPad and Mac can AirPlay to a zone or to “WavWiz – Everywhere.” Playing out to streamers and receivers with AirPlay or Cast is covered in Works with what you own.

  6. Spotify Connect, optional

    Off by default. Needs your own Spotify Premium account. Unofficial.

  7. Visualizers and themes

    Eight visualizers for a big screen, and ten themes or your own colors. See EQ & visualizers.

  8. Private

    No cloud account, no analytics, no telemetry, and WavWiz doesn’t open ports to the internet. Optional internet features (radio, covers, Spotify Connect) can be switched off. For access away from home, use your own Tailscale.

What’s new in v0.7.2 · Nov 21, 2026

Live sources, Announce in the Player, phone drag.

New in 0.7.2

Stream a PC's sound or mic to any zone

Only the Windows Player can send that PC's own sound (This PC · Speakers) or its microphone to any zone, the same way AirPlay already can.

  • Real time: the PC keeps playing with no extra lag, and the house follows. If that PC is in the same zone, WavWiz doesn't play the sound a second time there.
  • Sync with WavWiz: the PC's own speakers play in time with every zone. WavWiz moves Windows to a spare output for this — optical first, never a headset that's in use — and a tray item, Restore my speakers, puts your usual output back.
  • A calm stop: stopping PC audio asks first, with a one-tap Pause PC audio. When Sync hands back, the Windows volume is matched to what you heard, so nothing blares.

New in 0.7.2

Announce from the Windows Player, or a phone

Hold Announce to talk over the house. The music ducks, then fades back up when you let go. It works in the Windows Player window and in a browser on a phone.

  • Mic gain: microphone inputs go up to +24 dB, with a built-in limiter so a boosted mic doesn't clip.
  • Privacy: if Windows is blocking the microphone, the Player says so clearly and offers an Open microphone settings button.
  • Phone dragDrag an album, song or station onto Now Playing to play it now. Drag speakers between zones. Hold still for the menu; a drag won't select text by accident.
  • Gradient edge lightThe whole zone edge cross-fades through your theme colors. Heartbeat and Heartthrob are new too, and they replace the older comet, pulse and streak styles.
  • Volume mixerMaster volume now opens Devices in a Volume Mixer mode: every speaker on one line, in the same order as Devices.
  • Check with phoneA zone's Timing vs house row can measure how two zones line up where you stand, then suggest a timing value. Only an admin can apply it.
  • Add speakerAirPlay and Cast no longer sit in separate cards. One Add speaker button opens WavWiz, Browser, AirPlay and Cast tabs. (This landed in 0.7.1, which wasn't a public release.)
  • Live limiterA limiter after the AirPlay and Spotify Connect boost keeps loud tracks from clipping. Live sources use it too.

From 0.7.0

Google Cast speakers join your zones

Streamers and receivers with Google Cast show up in a Cast speakers card, right below AirPlay speakers in Devices.

  • Find, test, add: play a test chime, then add the speaker to a zone. It gets volume, mute, on/off, delay and Forget like any speaker, and reconnects after a restart.
  • Add by IP address: for a Cast device the network search can’t see, such as one on another subnet or VLAN.
  • Honest limits: new and still being tested on real Cast hardware. Sync with other speakers is close but approximate, so fine-tune it with the speaker’s delay. Calibrate speakers doesn’t cover Cast speakers yet.

From 0.7.0

An ARM64 Linux server, made for Raspberry Pi

The headless server now also builds for 64-bit ARM, as a linux-arm64 download and in a multi-arch Docker image (amd64 + arm64).

  • Raspberry Pi target: intended for Pi 4 and Pi 5 on 64-bit Raspberry Pi OS. Not yet verified on real Raspberry Pi hardware; so far it has been tested under emulation only.
  • Local audio on Linux: the server can play through the machine’s own headphone jack, HDMI or USB DAC, with volume, delay and EQ like any speaker. Also not yet verified on a real Pi.
  • Not on Linux yet: no AirPlay receiver (still planned), CD ripper or Spotify Connect.
  • AirPlay 2 fixesSetup now always opens the narrow PTP timing rule, fixing TVs that showed the song title but stayed at 0:00 with no sound. AirPlay 2 output is still experimental and off by default.
  • LG webOS TVsWavWiz automatically switches LG webOS TVs to realtime AirPlay mode instead of buffered AAC.
  • Radio logos and song titlesAirPlay screens show the station’s logo, and the song a station names shows in the mini player, Queue, Devices and on the phone.
  • Parallel Flow in SetupThe installer’s wizard uses the new Parallel Flow WavWiz emblem.
  • Zone clicks and Add toClicking a zone switches Now Playing and the Queue to it, the zone name opens its settings, and the Add-to-zone dropdown stays open across a refresh.
  • AirPlay card and restoresThe AirPlay speakers card no longer sticks on “Looking…” after a reload, and restoring a backup onto a fresh install keeps zones playable.

From 0.6.8

AirPlay setup and one reference per zone

Adding a speaker and lining up timing are both clearer in Devices.

Since 0.5.0

An AirPlay speaker can now be a WavWiz output. Add it to a zone and it follows play, pause, seek, skip and group moves with the rest of the house, with its own volume and delay.

Roadmap

Where WavWiz is headed.

The goal: every speaker, old or new, in one synced mesh. Next up is audiophile accuracy and an official Linux Player. Everything below that isn’t marked shipped is planned, not available yet, and there are no dates.

Out now in 0.7.2: live sources, Announce and phone drag. See What’s new. AirPlay 2 output is experimental, Google Cast is still in testing, and the ARM64 server is not yet verified on real Raspberry Pi hardware.

Coming next in 0.7.3 (planned)

Auto pre-gain and Direct mode

Cleaner, more accurate playback so boosts don't cost you volume, and a way to switch off all of WavWiz's processing.

  • Auto pre-gain: a planned fix so EQ, Bass/Treble boosts and Loudness no longer make the music quieter.
  • Direct (no processing): a planned switch that bypasses all of WavWiz's own processing (EQ, Bass/Treble and Loudness).

Upcoming in 0.8.0 (planned)

Linux Player and acoustic accuracy

The goal: every speaker, old or new, in one synced mesh. A planned official Player for Linux and Raspberry Pi, plus tools to measure and correct each speaker in the room.

  • Linux Player: an official Linux Player app (x64 and arm64) that pairs with a WavWiz server and shows up as a zone, like the Windows Player.
  • DLNA / UPnP renderer output: planned support so DLNA or UPnP renderers can join a zone. Not available yet.
  • Parametric EQ: exact frequency, gain and Q per band, with REW (Room EQ Wizard) filter import.
  • Per-speaker room correction: use a microphone to correct each speaker for the room.
  • Convolution / FIR: load professional room-correction filter files.
  • Equal-loudness: standards-based equal-loudness curves for Loudness.
  • True bit-perfect playback: native sample rate with exclusive output, so the file reaches the DAC untouched.

Shipped · 0.6.0

Linux & Docker

Run the WavWiz server on almost any always-on box, not just a Windows PC.

  • Linux server: headless WavWiz Server for 64-bit Intel/AMD (linux/amd64), so a NAS or x86-64 mini PC can run the house. Since 0.7.0: a 64-bit ARM build (linux/arm64) intended for Raspberry Pi 4/5, published but not yet verified on real Raspberry Pi hardware.
  • Docker: an official server image, now on Docker Hub (vdubbin74/wavwiz-server:0.7.2 and :latest, multi-arch amd64 + arm64). See the Docker guide for compose, including a Windows Docker Desktop walkthrough.
  • Remote libraries: play from a Subsonic or OpenSubsonic server, with Navidrome tested first.
  • Home Assistant: let Home Assistant control playback with a secure token.
  • Nothing to relearn: the same web app, zones, scenes and schedules.

Docker is a server only. Pair a Windows Player for PC speakers. See the Docker guide for compose and a Windows Docker Desktop walkthrough.

Docker limits vs the Windows app

Docker has the web UI, zones, scenes, schedules, alarms, Jukebox and Remote, and plays to Windows Players, phones and browsers, Cast and AirPlay 1 speakers, and (since 0.7.0) the host’s own sound card via /dev/snd. It does not have a microphone on the Docker host, an AirPlay receiver (still planned), CD ripping, Windows share logins, a Linux Player, or AirPlay 2 or HomePod output. On Windows Docker Desktop, discovery is weaker (no host networking); use published ports. Full limits list.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is WavWiz really free?

Yes. WavWiz is free and open source under the MIT license. There's nothing to buy, no subscription and no account to create.

Can my phone be a speaker? Can it play to a Bluetooth speaker?

Yes. Open the WavWiz page in your phone’s browser on your home Wi-Fi and tap Play music here. No app to install. If the phone is paired with a Bluetooth speaker, the music comes out there. Bluetooth adds lag, so give that phone a delay. On iPhone, tap the page once to start the sound and keep it open. See Works with what you own.

Can I put WavWiz on my home screen like an app?

Yes. First open the WavWiz page from your server on your phone or tablet. iPhone or iPad (Safari): tap Share, then Add to Home Screen, then Add. Android (Chrome): tap the three-dot menu, then Add to Home screen or Install app. You get a WavWiz shortcut that opens straight to your house, so you don't have to remember the address.

Can I use old PCs or laptops as speakers?

Yes. Any Windows 10 or 11 PC can run WavWiz Player and play through whatever output Windows can use. A Mac, Chromebook or older laptop can join in a browser. See Works with what you own.

Does it keep devices in sync?

Every device plays the same stream against a shared clock. Bluetooth speakers and TVs add lag, so each device gets its own delay (0 to 1000 ms). Set it by ear, or let Calibrate speakers measure a WavWiz Player with a PC mic or phone. AirPlay and Cast can’t be auto-calibrated yet. No sync measurements have been published yet.

Do I need special hardware?

No. You need one always-on Windows PC (or an optional Linux or Docker server) and the speakers you already have. See Works with what you own.

Does it work with Bluetooth, HDMI and receivers?

Yes, through a Windows PC running WavWiz Player: HDMI, optical, USB or Bluetooth, plus any other output Windows can play to. Streamers and receivers with Google Cast can join a zone (since 0.7.0, still in testing). Streamers and receivers with AirPlay can be added over AirPlay (AirPlay 2 is experimental and off by default). HomePod and Apple TV are not supported. DLNA / UPnP renderer output is planned for 0.8.0 and is not available yet. See Works with what you own.

Can guests add songs?

Yes. Anyone on your network can add songs from their own phone. For parties, Jukebox mode puts a QR code on a big screen. Guests scan it and add songs with no app and no sign-in, within your rules: allowed music, songs per guest, vote to skip and more. The Jukebox screen runs in a web browser on a PC, laptop or tablet. A PC or laptop connected to the TV works best; smart-TV browsers are still in testing.

Can I cap the volume in a kid's room?

Yes. As an admin, set a max-volume lock on any device. Nothing can raise that device past the cap, and turning it down always works.

What do I need to run it?

The server needs a 64-bit Windows 10 (October 2018 Update or newer) or Windows 11 PC that stays on, and everything else comes in the installer. Speakers need WavWiz Player on Windows or any modern browser on the same home network.

What doesn't work yet?

WavWiz is a public beta. AirPlay 2 output is experimental and off by default. Google Cast output (since 0.7.0) is still in testing. Calibrate speakers can’t measure AirPlay or Cast yet; set those delays by ear. The Linux/Docker arm64 server, intended for Raspberry Pi 4 and 5, has not been verified on real Pi hardware, and there is no official Linux or Pi Player yet (planned for 0.8.0). There is no AirPlay receiver on Linux yet (still planned). HomePod and Apple TV are not supported. DLNA / UPnP renderer output is planned for 0.8.0 and is not available yet. Smart-TV browsers are still in testing; a PC connected to the TV is the reliable path. On iPhone, tap the page once to start the sound. See the roadmap and Works with what you own.

Why does Windows SmartScreen warn me when I install WavWiz?

The installer isn't code-signed yet, so SmartScreen doesn't recognize it. If you downloaded it from the official WavWiz releases page on GitHub, click More info, then Run anyway. To double-check the file, compare its SHA-256 checksum with the one on the release page.

Who makes WavWiz, and was it built with AI?

WavWiz is a hobby project by vdubbin74, who builds his own speakers and keeps an always-on Windows PC at home. He came up with WavWiz, set the spec, makes the design decisions and tests the builds at home. AI coding agents wrote the code, and AI helped write the docs and this site. The source is on GitHub under the MIT license.

Where do I report bugs or ask questions?

Report bugs on GitHub Issues. For questions, ideas and setup help, use WavWiz Discussions on GitHub.
