Zones

Every room, its own music.

Multi-room

A different playlist in every zone

Group your speakers into zones like Kitchen, Basement or Office. Each zone can play its own music from its own queue, while the rest of the house keeps playing something else.

  • Pick a zone’s queue: the Queue dropdown shows any zone’s queue, on the PC and on your phone, and marks the one that’s playing.
  • Send music anywhere: drag songs from the Library into any zone’s queue, or copy a queue to another zone.
  • See every room: each zone shows what it’s playing, with its own volume.

Or all together

Link zones for one sound

Link zones and they share one queue, playing in sync. Split a zone off when it wants its own music, or use Link all to bring the whole house back together.

AirPlay to any zone, or everywhere. From an iPhone, iPad or Mac, pick “WavWiz – <zone>” to play in just that zone, or “WavWiz – Everywhere” to play in every zone at once. Turn on phone casting per zone in the zone editor.

  • Scenes and schedules: a scene saves your zones and how they’re linked. Schedules and alarms start music in the zone you choose.
  • Jukebox zone: give guest requests their own speakers.
  • One mixer: the Master Volume mixer shows every zone and speaker in one window.

Features

What it does

  1. Devices stay in sync

    Every device plays the same stream against a shared clock, and WavWiz keeps correcting each device's drift. Master volume, Mute all, and a 10-band EQ per speaker. The Master Volume mixer shows every speaker’s volume in one place, next to the master slider.

  2. Per-device delay

    Bluetooth speakers and TV audio run late. Give each device its own 0–1000 ms delay and adjust it live, or measure it with a PC mic (recommended) or your phone as the microphone (needs a one-time HTTPS setup; iPhone is still in testing).

  3. Plays on what you own

    Windows PCs play through their own speakers (wired, HDMI or Bluetooth) with WavWiz Player. Phones, tablets and Macs open a web page and join as a speaker automatically, with no app to install. On a phone, the remote opens full screen.

  4. AirPlay built in

    iPhone, iPad and Mac see “WavWiz – Everywhere” for every zone, and each zone can have its own speaker name too. Title and artwork come along.

  5. Spotify Connect, optional

    Off by default. Needs your own Spotify Premium account. Unofficial.

  6. Your library and radio

    Local folders and NAS shares, album pages and favorites, search and browse by artist, album, genre or folder, playlists, gapless albums, ReplayGain/R128 volume leveling, an internet radio finder, and an optional Studio Max 96 kHz streaming setting.

  7. One house, one queue

    Everyone can add songs from their own phone, and you can drag songs to reorder the playlist or drop them in from the Library. Group devices into zones, save scenes, and start them on a schedule, or run one from File › Run. A Jukebox zone lets you pick which speakers play guest requests.

  8. CD ripping and tag help

    Rip CDs to FLAC on the server PC. WavWiz looks up missing tags and covers on MusicBrainz and writes nothing until you approve it.

  9. Visualizers and themes

    Eight visualizers for a big screen. Ten themes, or pick your own colors.

  10. Private

    No cloud account, no analytics, no telemetry, and WavWiz doesn't open ports to the internet. Internet radio, tag and cover lookups, online album covers and artist photos (on by default; switch them off in Settings › Library), and optional Spotify Connect reach the internet. For access away from home, use your own Tailscale.

Devices

Use the speakers you already own.

  • Wired and USB speakersPlug them into a PC that runs WavWiz Player and pick the output. Each device shows what it plays through.
  • HDMI and AV receiversSend a PC's HDMI output to a TV or receiver.
  • Bluetooth speakersPair one to a PC. Bluetooth adds lag, so give that device a little delay or calibrate it to line it up.
  • Other PCsAny Windows 10 or 11 PC running WavWiz Player plays through its own speakers.
  • Phones and tabletsOpen the page in a browser to play as a speaker, or just to control the music without playing. Smart-TV browsers are still in testing, so a PC plugged into the TV is the surest bet.

Beta

Jukebox mode

Turn a PC into a locked party screen. Guests scan a QR code and add songs from their own phone, with no app and no sign-in. They see big cover art, what's playing and who asked for each song, with the visualizer behind it.

  • Your rules: pick the allowed folders and playlists, set songs per guest (3 by default), and turn on vote to skip, an artist cooldown or an explicit filter.
  • Never silent: auto-fill keeps music going when the queue runs dry.
  • Host stays in charge: pin a song next, remove a song, ban a guest or lock the queue. Only your host PIN leaves the Jukebox screen.
  • Two looks: Modern or Retro CD.
  • Any big screen: start it on your PC, then open the big screen on a spare PC, laptop or tablet from the “Jukebox on” button, or plug a PC into the TV. Guests keep queuing from their phones. Smart-TV browsers are still in testing.

The heart of WavWiz

Every device, in sync

One stream, one shared clock, every device playing together. No echo between devices, just one sound through the whole house.

  • Calibrate each device: measure each device's delay with a PC mic or your phone's mic, or set it by ear (0–1000 ms).
  • One reference speaker: mark a REF device and every other delay lines up to it.
  • Any output: HDMI to a TV or receiver, optical, wired or Bluetooth. Each PC keeps a delay per output.
  • Stays locked: WavWiz keeps correcting each device's drift while the music plays.

How it works

Three steps to whole-home sound.

  1. 01

    Set up one PC

    Install WavWiz Server on a Windows PC that stays on and set an admin password. Then point it at your music in Settings › Library: a local folder or a NAS share like \\NAS\Music.

  2. 02

    Add devices

    Old PCs and laptops run WavWiz Player; phones and tablets just open the page, no app needed. Each one plays through whatever it's connected to: HDMI, a receiver or a Bluetooth speaker.

  3. 03

    Line up and play

    Calibrate once with a PC mic or your phone so every device lines up, group devices into zones, and play on every device in sync. Having people over? Hand the queue to Jukebox mode.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is WavWiz really free?

Yes. WavWiz is free and open source under the MIT license. There's nothing to buy, no subscription and no account to create.

Can my phone be a speaker? Can it play to a Bluetooth speaker?

Yes. Open the WavWiz page in your phone's browser on your home Wi-Fi and tap Play music here. There's no app to install. The phone plays through whatever it's using for sound, so if it's paired with a Bluetooth speaker, headphones or a home AV receiver with Bluetooth, the music comes out there. Tablets work the same way. Bluetooth adds its own lag, so give that phone a little delay (or calibrate it) to line it up with the other devices. On iPhone, tap the page once to start the sound, and keep the WavWiz page open while it plays.

Can I put WavWiz on my home screen like an app?

Yes. First open the WavWiz page from your server on your phone or tablet. iPhone or iPad (Safari): tap Share, then Add to Home Screen, then Add. Android (Chrome): tap the three-dot menu, then Add to Home screen or Install app. You get a WavWiz shortcut that opens straight to your house, so you don't have to remember the address.

Can I use old PCs or laptops as speakers?

Yes. Any Windows 10 or 11 PC can run WavWiz Player and play through its own speakers, headphones, HDMI, optical, USB or Bluetooth output. Anything else with a modern browser, like a Mac, a Chromebook or an older laptop, can join by opening the WavWiz page.

Does it keep devices in sync?

Every device plays the same stream against a shared clock, and WavWiz keeps correcting each device's drift so devices stay together. Bluetooth speakers and TVs add their own lag, so each device gets its own delay (0 to 1000 ms). Set it by ear while the music plays, or let Calibrate speakers measure it with a PC mic or your phone. No sync measurements have been published yet.

Do I need special hardware?

No. You need one Windows PC that stays on to run the server, and whatever speakers you already have: PCs, laptops, phones, tablets, and the speakers, TVs and receivers they connect to.

Does it work with Bluetooth, HDMI and receivers?

Yes, through a PC. WavWiz Player plays to any output Windows can use, including HDMI to a TV or receiver, optical, USB DACs and Bluetooth speakers, and each speaker shows which output it's using. WavWiz can't send audio to Sonos, Chromecast or DLNA speakers directly.

Can guests add songs?

Yes. Anyone on your network can add songs from their own phone. For parties, Jukebox mode puts a QR code on a big screen. Guests scan it and add songs with no app and no sign-in, within your rules: allowed music, songs per guest, vote to skip and more. The Jukebox screen runs in a web browser on a PC, laptop or tablet. A PC or laptop connected to the TV works best; smart-TV browsers are still in testing.

Can I cap the volume in a kid's room?

Yes. As an admin, set a max-volume lock on any device. Nothing can raise that device past the cap, and turning it down always works.

What do I need to run it?

The server needs a 64-bit Windows 10 (October 2018 Update or newer) or Windows 11 PC that stays on, and everything else comes in the installer. Speakers need WavWiz Player on Windows or any modern browser on the same home network.

What doesn't work yet?

WavWiz is a public beta. Smart-TV browsers are still in testing: WavWiz gives TVs a lighter layout and visualizer, but some (including an older LG webOS set) don't finish loading yet. For now, the reliable way to use a TV is a PC or laptop connected to it, and if you try your TV's browser, we'd love to hear how it went. The server runs only on 64-bit Windows 10 or 11, with no Mac or Linux server and no Docker image. AirPlay shows up as one whole-house speaker, not one per device, and it's classic AirPlay, so iPhone won't group it with AirPlay 2 speakers. WavWiz can't play to Sonos, Chromecast or DLNA speakers. On iPhone, tap the page once to start the sound.

Why does Windows SmartScreen warn me when I install WavWiz?

The installer isn't code-signed yet, so SmartScreen doesn't recognize it. If you downloaded it from the official WavWiz releases page on GitHub, click More info, then Run anyway. To double-check the file, compare its SHA-256 checksum with the one on the release page.

Who makes WavWiz, and was it built with AI?

WavWiz is a hobby project by vdubbin74, who builds his own speakers and keeps an always-on Windows PC at home. He came up with WavWiz, set the spec, makes the design decisions and tests the builds at home. AI coding agents wrote the code, and AI helped write the docs and this site. The source is on GitHub under the MIT license.

Where do I report bugs or ask questions?

Report bugs on GitHub Issues. For questions, ideas and setup help, use WavWiz Discussions on GitHub.
